# Direct, useful documentation

Write so readers can understand, decide, or act and get back to work.

## Adapt to the project

Derive language, tone, audience, and conventions from project instructions and existing
documentation. Adapt terminology and examples to the business context: users, roles, workflows,
and actual product guarantees. Do not impose the source project's language or promises.

## Criteria

- Start with what the reader needs: what to do, what happens, or which limit matters.
- Use familiar words, direct verbs, and short paragraphs. Keep the tone casual, friendly, and
  human, without ceremony, forced humor, or textbook introductions.
- In product introductions and READMEs, highlight concrete benefits with restrained, factual
  marketing. Avoid unsupported superlatives, promises, and comparisons. Prioritize practical
  information in guides and references.
- Give each fact one authoritative location. Link to it from other documents rather than
  repeating instructions, defaults, or explanations.
- Use tables for comparisons or lookup information and steps for sequences. Keep a paragraph
  as a paragraph when a table or list would add no value.
- Add examples only when they clarify usage. Choose the smallest useful example.
- Preserve prerequisites, limits, and warnings that change a decision or prevent errors.
  Brevity does not mean omitting necessary information or writing in fragments.
- Check claims against code, tests, or official sources. Distinguish supported, tested, and
  pending behavior; do not promise parity without evidence.
- Edit the document that already owns the topic. Split by responsibility only when it helps
  readers find information or maintain it; do not create files by habit.

## Before delivery

Remove sentences that add no action, fact, concrete benefit, or necessary clarification.
Check links, commands, and consistency with existing references. Do not add a summary that
repeats what the reader just read.

Tone example:

- Avoid: “In order to take advantage of this functionality, it is necessary to install the runtime beforehand.”
- Prefer: “Install the runtime before invoking the function.”
